What Comes After Phase 1

This program is Phase 1: the foundation before spending money in the U.S. market. Now it is time to decide what comes next. Some business owners move forward independently. Others benefit from the next stages of the UCF BIP market entry/soft landing program:

  • Personalized advisory. One-on-one guidance specific to your business
  • Professional connections. Warm introductions to attorneys, CPAs, and banking contacts
  • Ecosystem access. Investors, industry contacts, and partner organizations
  • Peer community. Other international founders at a similar stage
  • Structured milestones. Progress tracking with regular check-ins

Details are discussed during your 1:1 meeting. Your conversation will help determine which path makes sense.

Your Top 3 Actions for the Next 30 Days

These are your first three moves after completing this program. Pick your top three actions for the next 30 days. Three, not ten. Specific actions lead to higher follow-through than long lists.

Make Each Action Specific

Weak: "Learn more about entity types." Strong: "Schedule a consultation with a business attorney to discuss LLC vs. C-Corp for my visa situation by [date]."

High-impact 30-day actions:

  1. Book your 1:1 with UCF BIP. Do it today.
  2. Contact a specific professional. Attorney, CPA, or immigration lawyer. "Email this referral by Friday."
  3. Complete one research task. SunBiz name check, USPTO search, or compile bank documentation.
  4. Register for a free resource. SBDC counseling, SCORE mentorship, or a chamber event.
  5. Complete the Expansion Decision Worksheet. Block 60 minutes before your 1:1.

Important Reminder Before You Go

This program provides general educational information to help international founders understand the U.S. business environment. It does not constitute legal, tax, immigration, or financial advice. Founders should consult qualified professionals before making business decisions.

This is not a formality. The information in this program gives you a strong foundation, but every founder's case is different. Your entity choice, tax duties, visa pathway, and IP strategy depend on your specific situation. The experts you work with will tailor the general knowledge here to your business, country of origin, industry, and goals. That is how knowledge becomes safe action.

Check Your Understanding

What Would You Do?

Question 1 of 3

An entrepreneur has completed Phase 1 (this program), landed her first two U.S. customers, and generated early revenue. Which UCF BIP phase is designed for her current business stage?

Phase 3 of the UCF BIP market entry/soft landing program is specifically designed for entrepreneurs who already have initial traction: early customers, revenue, or signed partnerships. It focuses on growth strategy, investor connections, expanded networking, and operational support for scaling. Phase 2 is for founders still establishing initial operations.

According to the lesson's guidance on effective goal-setting, which of the following is the STRONGEST action item for a founder's 30-day plan?

Effective action items must be specific, time-bound, and completable. 'Schedule a 30-minute consultation with a CPA who works with foreign-owned businesses by March 28' includes a specific action (schedule consultation), a specific qualifier (CPA with international experience), and a deadline. Vague goals like 'learn more' or 'research the market' lack the specificity needed for follow-through.

A founder from Guatemala has completed this Phase 1 program and wants to take action this week. She has not yet formed a U.S. entity, has no SBDC appointment, and has not completed the Expansion Decision Worksheet. Based on the lesson's guidance on prioritizing next steps, which specific set of actions should she take in the next seven days?

The lesson emphasizes three specific first-week actions: book the UCF BIP 1:1 meeting (free, 30 minutes, the single most important next step), connect with a support resource like SBDC, and complete the Expansion Decision Worksheet so the BIP team can give targeted advice. Filing an LLC before consulting a professional risks choosing the wrong entity type. Waiting for full confidence delays progress unnecessarily. Networking matters, but structured support should come first.
