Did You Know?

The UCF Business Incubation Program operates multiple sites across the greater Orlando region, providing over 100,000 square feet of incubation space. Since its founding, companies in the program have created thousands of jobs in Central Florida. UCF itself is one of the top patent-producing universities in the country, and the research partnerships between campus labs and incubator companies give founders access to talent and technology that would cost a fortune to build independently.

Resource 1: U.S. Market Entry Checklist

This is your comprehensive checklist, every item, verified. The U.S. Market Entry Checklist turns every major topic from this program into action items grouped by module: legal setup, finances, IP protection, team building, market entry, culture, and ecosystem resources. Each item includes a priority level and suggested timeframe.

Tip: Start with the ecosystem section. It connects you with free resources before you start paying for services.

Resource 2: Florida Setup Roadmap

This is your step-by-step sequence, what comes first, second, third. The Florida Setup Roadmap is sorted by order instead of topic, answering exactly that question. It walks through four phases: (1) pre-arrival preparation (remote), (2) entity formation and registration, (3) financial infrastructure, and (4) operational readiness. Each phase includes estimated timeframes and flags which steps require physical presence versus remote completion.

Many founders complete phases 1 and 2 remotely before arriving, so they can open a bank account in one visit with all documents ready.

Resource 3: Central Florida Resource Guide

This guide puts every support organization one call away. The Central Florida Resource Guide puts all the groups from Module 7 into a single, easy-to-search reference. It covers business advising (SBDC, SCORE, UCF BIP (opens in a new tab)), incubators and accelerators, government programs (SBA, Enterprise Florida), professional services (attorneys, CPAs, banking contacts), networking and community organizations, and details on what comes after this course through UCF BIP (opens in a new tab).

Each entry includes the organization name, what they offer, and their website. Bookmark it and update it as you discover new resources through networking.

Resource 4: Expansion Decision Worksheet

This is your go/no-go decision, the honest assessment before you commit. The Expansion Decision Worksheet helps you decide whether to enter the U.S. market now. It scores you across five areas: market fit, financial readiness, legal readiness, operations, and timing.

This is not pass/fail. A business owner from Colombia scored 2 out of 5 on financial readiness, spent four months strengthening home-market revenue, re-scored at 4, and entered the U.S. with a much stronger foundation. The worksheet tells you what gap to close first.

Complete this before your 1:1 meeting with UCF BIP (opens in a new tab). It helps the team give you targeted advice based on your specific readiness profile.

Question 1 of 3

A founder has validated her market opportunity and wants to know the correct ORDER of steps to become operational in Florida, from entity formation through financial setup to hiring. Which downloadable resource is specifically designed for this purpose?

The Florida Setup Roadmap is organized by sequence rather than by topic. It walks entrepreneurs through four phases: pre-arrival preparation, entity formation, financial infrastructure, and operational readiness, in the correct order. The Checklist organizes by module topic, while the Roadmap answers 'what do I do first, second, third?'

A founder completed the Expansion Decision Worksheet and scored high on market fit but low on financial readiness. Based on the lesson guidance, what is the most strategic response?

The Expansion Decision Worksheet is not a pass/fail test. A founder from Colombia used it, found her financial score was 2 out of 5, spent four months strengthening home-market revenue, and re-scored at 4 before entering the U.S. with a stronger foundation. The worksheet tells you what gap to close first, not whether to give up.

A founder has completed this program and is preparing to meet with the UCF BIP team. What is the best way to make the most of the 1:1 meeting?

The 1:1 meeting with the UCF BIP team works best when you arrive with the Expansion Decision Worksheet completed and specific questions ready. That lets the team give focused advice based on your specific readiness profile rather than starting from scratch. The meeting is to guide you, not to pitch your product or have the team make decisions for you.
