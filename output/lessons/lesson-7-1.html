Business Support Organizations Available to Help You

Your Support Network: Where Everything Connects

Central Florida has a business support network that many international founders never discover on their own. SBDC, SBA, chambers of commerce, UCF BIP, SCORE, Prospera: each one connects to the others, and they are all here to support founders like you.

Many international founders spend thousands on private consultants for services available free through these organizations.

The U.S. has a network of business support groups funded by federal, state, and local government. Most welcome foreign founders, many have bilingual staff, and almost all are free or low-cost. You are not asking for charity. These resources exist for founders like you.

Free Help Is Closer Than You Think

A business owner from Colombia spent $4,500 on a private consultant to help navigate U.S. business setup, then discovered that the Small Business Development Center at UCF offers the same guidance for free. Central Florida has several organizations specifically designed to help international founders, and most of them cost nothing.

Watch Your Money: What This Actually Costs

Support organization costs as of early 2026:

  • SBDC business counseling: Free (unlimited sessions)
  • SBDC workshops: Free to $50 per session
  • SBA.gov resources and guides: Free
  • Orlando Economic Partnership consultation: Free
  • Chamber of Commerce membership: $200–$500/year
  • SCORE mentoring: Free (volunteer business mentors)
  • Prospera programs: Free to low-cost

Compare that to private help: $150–$350/hour for a business consultant, or $3,000–$10,000 for a market entry strategy that the SBDC develops with you at no cost. Always check the free options first.

Common Mistakes International Founders Make: Resources

Consider these common pitfalls:

  • Not knowing about free resources (SBDC, SBA counseling). Many international founders assume quality business advice costs money. The U.S. has an entire taxpayer-funded system built to help you for free.
  • Paying consultants for advice available through government programs. Before you hire any consultant, check if the SBDC or SBA covers that topic first. You might save $5,000 or more.
  • Not leveraging UCF BIP and chamber connections. These organizations exist to connect you with the right people. A warm introduction from UCF BIP or a chamber contact carries more weight than a cold email.

What Would You Do?

Question 1 of 3

Which organization provides free, ongoing one-on-one business counseling to international founders in Central Florida?

The Florida SBDC (Small Business Development Center) at UCF provides free one-on-one business counseling. This is an ongoing relationship, not a one-time meeting. Advisors help with business planning, market research, financial analysis, and strategy at no cost.

An international founder wants help with market research and a business plan. What should they do FIRST?

The SBDC should be your first stop for market research and business planning support. Their advisors provide exactly this service at no cost. Hiring a consultant for the same work could cost $3,000–$10,000. Always check free resources before paying for private help.

An entrepreneur from Peru just arrived in Central Florida. She needs help with market research, a business plan, and connections to potential customers. She has $8,000 set aside for consulting fees. Based on how the support ecosystem works, what should she expect when she engages with these organizations?

The support ecosystem is interconnected but each organization serves a distinct role. The SBDC provides free, unlimited business counseling including market research and business plans. SCORE offers free mentorship from experienced executives. Chambers of commerce provide networking for a modest annual fee ($200–$500). These services are available to international founders regardless of citizenship status. The $8,000 she budgeted for consulting could be redirected to actual business operations.

Key Takeaway

Central Florida's support ecosystem is deep, well-organized, and interconnected. The SBDC at UCF should be your first call for business planning and market research. The SBA offers federal resources and guides. Chambers of commerce provide networking and credibility. Use these resources before you spend money on private consultants. Compare the free options to paid ones, then see which groups match your stage. The founders who succeed fastest engage the ecosystem and use every available advantage.
