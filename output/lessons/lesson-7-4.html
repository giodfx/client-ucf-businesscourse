UCF BIP Programs: Your Next Step

Following the Current Forward, Your Pathway Through UCF BIP

You have worked through every major topic in this program: entity setup, finance, IP, hiring, product adaptation, culture, and regional resources. The question now: what concrete support is available next? UCF BIP helps international founders turn knowledge into action. This Phase 1 program is the introduction. The hands-on support comes in phases 2 and 3.

Phases 2 and 3 of the UCF BIP Market Entry / Soft Landing Program

Let's break this down. Phase 2 is for founders who have validated their market opportunity and are ready to establish initial U.S. operations:

  • One-on-one mentorship with industry-experienced advisors
  • UCF BIP workspace and professional business address
  • Warm introductions to customers, partners, and service providers
  • Advanced workshops: investor readiness, sales strategy, regulatory compliance
  • Peer community of international founders at a similar stage

Phase 3 is for founders with initial traction: early customers, revenue, or a signed partnership:

  • Growth strategy development with experienced advisors
  • Investor community connections (when ready)
  • Expanded networking through BIP's alumni network and partners
  • Operational support for hiring, legal, and financial infrastructure
  • Continued workspace and resource access

The transition between phases adapts to your pace. The goal is progress, not speed.

Success Stories: Companies That Benefited from UCF BIP

International founders have used UCF BIP to launch and grow U.S. operations across a range of industries. Here is a recent graduate of the UCF BIP Soft Landing Program facing a familiar crossroads:

Case Study: Freshorize

Founded: 2002  ·  Industry: Airline hygiene & wellbeing products  ·  Products: Hand soaps, lotions, sanitizers, aircraft interior cleaners, wellbeing line

Freshorize had strong success in Europe but hit a ceiling. The EU airline supply market was mature and saturated, meaningful growth was stalling, and the largest opportunities sat with U.S. carriers. The problem: no U.S. customers, no procurement track record with American airlines, and no warm introductions. They needed a way in.

Step Into Freshorize's Shoes

It's 2020. You run Freshorize — a European airline hygiene company founded in 2002. Your products are strong, your European brand is solid, but the EU market is saturated and growth has stalled. You want into the U.S. aviation market where the largest airlines are, but you have no U.S. customers, no procurement track record with American airlines, and no warm introductions. What is your next move?

What Freshorize Actually Did, and What It Yielded

Freshorize chose the program path. With UCF BIP support, they:

  • Refined positioning and supplier credentials for U.S. airline procurement standards
  • Got warm introductions to procurement decision-makers at major U.S. carriers
  • Navigated early-stage trust barriers without burning through capital on cold outreach
  • Built a repeatable sales motion, not a one-off win

The results:

  • Secured contracts with major U.S. airlines including Delta Air Lines and United Airlines
  • Successfully expanded from a Europe-centric business into the U.S. aviation market
  • Established a scalable growth pathway beyond their original geography
  • Recently graduated from the UCF BIP Soft Landing Program

Key Takeaway: International growth is not just about having a strong product, it is about access, credibility, and the right entry system. The UCF BIP Soft Landing Program provided the bridge from "proven in Europe" to "trusted in the U.S. market."

Community Resources for International Founders

UCF BIP is one spring feeding into a much larger river. Several partner organizations work alongside it, each serving a different need. You do not have to use all of them. Pick the ones that match where your business is right now and save the rest for later.

Hispanic Chamber of Commerce of Metro Orlando

Membership costs $200–$400 per year and gives you access to networking events, business workshops, and advocacy. Monthly events connect you with established bilingual business owners and service providers who understand both the U.S. market and the Latin American business mindset. Many founders find their first local partner or customer referral through chamber connections.

Prospera

This nonprofit supports Hispanic entrepreneurs through one-on-one mentoring, business training, and access to micro-lending. Programs are free or low-cost. What makes it especially useful is that the mentors have navigated the same cultural and business transition you are going through. They understand the challenges firsthand.

CFITO (Central Florida International Trade Office)

If your business involves import, export, or cross-border trade between Latin America, the Caribbean, and the United States, this is the organization to contact. They run structured trade delegations that include market research, partner identification, and face-to-face meetings with buyers and distributors.

SCORE

A national program that provides free business mentoring from volunteer advisors, many of them retired executives with decades of experience. The Orlando chapter matches you with a mentor in your industry for ongoing coaching. Sessions are free and available in person or virtually.

Reduce Risk: Don't Go It Alone

The founders who succeed in the U.S. market use their support network. They join chambers, attend SBDC workshops, participate in UCF BIP advisory sessions, and connect with mentors through SCORE. The cost of joining is small. The cost of going alone is high. Founders who have been through it say the same thing: the network mattered as much as the knowledge.

Your Next Steps After This Program

Now, let's get concrete. You are almost finished with Phase 1. Here is what happens next:

Step 1: Complete the Self-Assessment (Module 8)

A structured self-assessment of your U.S. market readiness, not a test, but a tool to identify confident areas and gaps. The results guide your UCF BIP conversation.

Step 2: Schedule a 1:1 Meeting with UCF BIP

Your chance to:

  • Discuss your specific business and market entry plans
  • Ask about phases 2 and 3 of the market entry/soft landing program
  • Get tailored resource recommendations
  • Identify your three to five most important next steps

Step 3: Connect with Your Support Network

Before your 1:1, take one concrete action:

  • Schedule a free SBDC counseling session (sbdcorlando.com)
  • Register for a chamber or networking event
  • Contact CFITO about trade programs
  • Sign up for SCORE mentoring

Showing up already in motion helps the team advise you more effectively.

UCF BIP Application Readiness Checklist

Match Your Stage to the Right UCF BIP Phase

For founders evaluating whether to expand to the U.S.

Phase 1 is the program you are completing right now. It is designed for international founders who have a working business in their home country and are considering U.S. expansion.

What you get:

  • Self-paced online curriculum (8 modules, ~3 hours)
  • 1:1 strategy session with the UCF BIP team after completion
  • Access to Central Florida Resource Guide and decision worksheets
  • Bilingual support (English and Spanish)

Best fit: You are still validating whether U.S. expansion makes sense for your business. You want a structured way to surface the questions you have not yet answered.

Cost: No fee for Phase 1.

Outcome: A clear go/no-go decision, plus a prioritized list of gaps to close before you take the next step.

For founders who have decided to enter the U.S. and are setting up operations.

Phase 2 is hands-on incubation: you have a U.S. entity (or are forming one), and you are working through the operational steps that turn a plan into a running business in Central Florida.

What you get:

  • Dedicated incubation manager and quarterly business reviews
  • Discounted office space at one of UCF's incubator locations across Central Florida
  • Introductions to attorneys, accountants, banks, and service providers experienced with international founders
  • Access to UCF research, talent (interns, recent grads), and faculty consults
  • Workshops on hiring, sales, fundraising, and U.S. business culture

Best fit: You have validated your opportunity, you are setting up or have just set up your U.S. entity, and you need infrastructure plus a peer network.

Cost: Membership fee varies by site and stage. Contact your 1:1 advisor for current pricing.

Outcome: A functioning U.S. operation with a board of advisors and the operational infrastructure to scale.

For founders with U.S. revenue and product-market fit, ready to scale.

Phase 3 is for established UCF BIP companies that have moved beyond setup and are scaling their U.S. business: hiring teams, raising capital, expanding into multiple markets.

What you get:

  • Curated investor introductions (angel, VC, family offices)
  • Strategic advisors matched to your sector and stage
  • Access to UCF's broader corporate partner network (Lockheed, AdventHealth, Walt Disney, Siemens, and others)
  • Co-marketing opportunities with the regional economic development partners
  • Acceleration cohort programs (specific industry verticals or growth stages)

Best fit: You have $500K+ U.S. revenue, a working U.S. team, and a credible plan to grow significantly over the next 12-18 months.

Cost: Higher membership fee; some programs are equity-based or success-fee based.

Outcome: Capital raised, partnerships closed, and a clear runway to your next major milestone (Series A, profitability, or exit).

Question 1 of 3

What is the PRIMARY difference between Phase 2 and Phase 3 of the UCF BIP market entry/soft landing program?

Phase 2 is designed for founders who have validated their market opportunity and are ready to establish initial U.S. operations (workspace, mentorship, introductions). Phase 3 is for founders who already have early traction: customers, revenue, or partnerships, and need support scaling. The phases match different business stages, not different industries.

A Spanish-speaking entrepreneur from Ecuador wants free, bilingual mentoring and market research support tailored to Hispanic entrepreneurs. Which organization should she contact first?

Prospera is a nonprofit specifically dedicated to supporting Hispanic entrepreneurs in Florida. Their programs include one-on-one bilingual mentoring, business training workshops, and access to micro-lending programs. Their advisors understand the specific challenges Latin American founders face in the U.S. market, and services are free or low-cost.

A founder from Costa Rica has just finished this Phase 1 program and is eager to move forward. He has not yet contacted any local organizations. According to the lesson's guidance on next steps, what sequence of actions should he take to engage with UCF BIP most effectively?

The lesson outlines a three-step sequence: (1) complete the self-assessment in Module 8 to identify strengths and gaps, (2) take one concrete action such as scheduling SBDC counseling or registering for a networking event, and (3) bring those results to the 1:1 meeting with UCF BIP. Showing up already in motion helps the team advise more effectively. Phase 3 is for business owners with early traction, not pre-entry founders, and the 1:1 meeting is a key part of the process, not something to skip.
