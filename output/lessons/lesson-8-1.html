Self-Assessment: Are You Ready?

Self-Assessment: What You Now Know

Most founders don't ask themselves this until after they've already spent money: which parts of this system do I actually understand well enough to act on, and which parts are still unclear? Having information is not the same as being ready. This module is a structured self-assessment, a tool to identify your strongest areas and the gaps you still need to fill before committing time or money.

The Four Themes as Your Decision Filter

Before you sign anything, hire anyone, or transfer money, recall the four themes:

  • Reduce Risk. Avoid the legal, financial, and operational traps.
  • Watch Your Money. Know the realistic costs before committing.
  • Avoid Common Mistakes. Don't repeat the errors other founders make.
  • Understand the Culture. Know how American business actually works.

Important Reminder: This Was Educational Guidance

Everything you covered in this program was general educational information intended to help you understand the U.S. business environment. It is not legal, tax, immigration, or financial advice. Before you take action on any topic: entity formation, banking, taxes, IP, hiring, or visas, consult a qualified professional who can review your specific situation. Knowledge is the starting point. Professional guidance is how you turn knowledge into safe action.

Your Readiness Self-Assessment

Before you enter the U.S. market, you need a readiness review. This is yours. For each topic, honestly rate yourself: clear on what to do, or still have major questions? No passing score. The point is clarity.

Your Next Actions

    Where Do You Need More Help?

    No preparation is perfect. The smartest founders identify what is missing and get help before entering the market. Feeling swamped is normal: you are not supposed to be an expert in all seven areas. That is exactly why UCF has an entire ecosystem of advisors, mentors, and professional connections available to you. You need to know enough to ask the right questions and avoid expensive mistakes.

    Where to Get Help

    • Legal (entity, contracts, compliance). Business attorney with international experience. SBDC connects you with pro bono legal clinics.
    • Taxes and accounting. CPA with international experience. Ask: "Do you work with foreign-owned businesses?"
    • Immigration and visas. Immigration attorney. Not optional.
    • IP protection. IP attorney. USPTO has resources, but filing strategy needs professional guidance.
    • Banking. Start with the bank directly. Bring documents; expect the first visit to be a consultation.
    • Customer acquisition. SBDC and SCORE offer free advising. UCF BIP connects you with mentors.
    • General guidance. The UCF BIP team is available for a conversation once you complete Module 8.

    Your readiness scores are your priority list. Prioritize professionals based on your lowest scores.

    An entrepreneur rates herself a '2' on the Legal Setup section of the readiness self-assessment. According to the scoring guide, what should she do before taking action on entity formation?

    The readiness self-assessment scoring guide states that any area rated 1 or 2 is a priority for professional consultation before taking action. Getting entity selection wrong costs more than the filing fee. It can mean months of restructuring and potential tax complications.

    The program identifies four recurring themes that should guide every business decision. A founder is about to hire a private immigration consultant for $5,000. Which theme should she apply FIRST?

    The 'Watch Your Money' theme reminds business owners to check free resources before spending on private services. While immigration law specifically requires a licensed attorney (not SBDC), the broader principle applies: always verify whether government-funded programs or free advisory services cover your need before paying for private consulting.

    A founder from Chile rated herself 4 out of 5 on market research and product readiness, but 1 out of 5 on legal setup and 2 out of 5 on U.S. taxes. She wants to start selling to U.S. customers next month. Based on the readiness framework in this lesson, how should she evaluate whether her timeline is realistic?

    The readiness framework treats scores of 1 or 2 as priorities for professional consultation before taking action. Selling to U.S. customers without a properly formed entity, an understanding of tax obligations, or the right contracts creates liability exposure. Her strong market scores are valuable, but the legal and tax gaps must be addressed first. The solution is not to wait a year, it is to schedule consultations with a business attorney and CPA immediately, which could resolve the gaps in weeks rather than months.

    Key Takeaway

    You do not need to have everything figured out. You need to know what to figure out first and who can help. Your readiness self-assessment is not a test score, it is your priority checklist. The areas where you rated yourself lowest are your highest-priority next steps. Decide which gaps need professional guidance first, then create a specific action plan for each one. Use these priorities to guide your 1:1 conversation with the UCF BIP team.
